# General Info

Stained Mists is a roguelite perma-death RPG(given it was inspired by Deepwoken). Characters are temporary, and you are expected to lose characters.
Each character has 8 lives. Once they die 8 times, they wipe, and you have to start over.

# Skill Progression

Max level is 20. Each level currently gives 2 points(although this is prone to change with testing) which you can invest in skill trees. There are 3 types
of skill trees: Allomantic, Natural, and Weapons. Allomantic skill trees are related to your allomantic abilities. Weapon skill trees improve your specific weapon class
(dagger, sword, fist, etc.). Natural skill trees are what every character has, and they define the main direction of your build. Natural skill trees are: 
Fortitude, Agility, Strength, Stealth, and Focus.

Fortitude increases health, resistances, and general sustainability.

Agility increases speed, movement, dodges, etc.

Strength increases mainly blunt damage, alongside some other stuff like max posture.

Stealth decreses your odds of being seen by NPCs, but also has a side called Obfuscation. Obfuscation's goal is to make moves confusing and hard to read. The general direction of
Obfuscation-oriented build is to sacrafice damage and sustain to make hits easier to land.

Focus is the counter to Obfuscation. It improves you resistance to it, alongside increasing your odds of parrying. It is the most gimmicky skill tree of the five.

Each skill tree has Master Skills. These skills are only unlockable once you reach max level, and they cannot be unlocked by Mistborns(unless you are an original mistborn, in which case you can unlock 1
Master Skill). Additionally, there are Deep Master Skills, which are just Master Skills with a Master Skill requirement. This means they cannot be unlocked by Original Mistborns, only
Mistings with single metals. Since Master Skills are more powerful than normal skills, and Deep Master Skills are _even more_ powerful, this allows players to sacrifice Mistborn's
versatility of weilding all the metals for the sake of specializing in one metal, and making it powerful. Not only that, but since Mistborns have 8 allomantic skill trees to work with instead of 1,
it naturally gives Mistings more points to focus onto that one tree.

# Gear Progression

There are 4 main weapon materials in the game: Wood, Glass, Obsidian, and Steel, ordered from weakest to strongest. Wood is weak, but cheap. Glass is the middle ground, having decent damage, price,
and durability(yes, there is durability). Obsidian is more expensive, more damaging, but less durable version of glass. Steel has the highest durability, price, and damage, but comes with
a huge risk: Steel gear is rendered useless against Iron/Steel allomancer. They can practically stunlock a steel armor, and disarm a steel weapon. It can, however, be extremely useful when
facing a different kind of allomacer.

Armor works similarly, but has no durability, can have armorset skills, can have stats like health or agility, and isn't strictly bound to a set of materials.

Armors and Weapons also have Tiers from 0 to 3. They give additional stats like damage, health, resistance, stealth, etc.

# Crew/House

As each character has the chance to choose to be either Skaa or Nobility, they can form or join a Crew(for Skaa) or a House(for Nobles). This part of the game is still in the planning, but
here is the general rundown. Bases are a spot where you can find missions, run heists(skaa), make merchandise trades(nobility), go on expeditions(high risk high reward quests, involving permanent death),
store loot, mix metal vials(for ingesting metals), gear crafting, and more. Base raid are also envisioned where you could attack an enemy base to steal a part of their loot. Bases will also be highly customizable
and upgradable, including the ability to increase tresury security, hire guards and equip them with gear, add more rooms and change their styles, etc. Also, as this is your Base, everything that requires
payment will be cheaper here, and missions and heists will yield more. Bases are essentially an investment into not only a safehome, but also an investment to earn more money, securing stable
source of better gear and allomantic supplies.

# Mistborn Progression

To unlock Mistborn one must first master all 8 metals. That means creating a Misting character for each metal, and getting at least 1 master skill each time.

The reason I chose these Mistborn requirements are as follows:
- Players get to experience and focus on all 8 Misting classes individually, meaning they get to learn how to use them fully. For example, a Mistborn may only ever use Tin minimally, and fully focus
  on Steel and Iron. This way, they get to fully explore Tin and find its quirks they would never find otherwise.
- Each class has its own special missions, which is a big part of the experience.
- I want Mistborns to be rare. Canonically, Mistborns are meant to be very rare, and seeing everyone as a Mistborn would break the immersion. This is also why I gave my best ballancing individual
  Misting classes.
- If Mistborn was available right off the start, most players would instantly choose it over Mistings since it feels more special. This means they will likely not experience other playstyles, even though
  they may like it more. This way, they explore different ways to play the game.
- By learning how to play a certain class, they will learn its strengths and weaknesses, meaning they will have better understanding playing against those classes once they meet them later in the game.
- Obtaining a Mistborn should feel like a goal, and should feel rewarding. It makes it that much more satisfying to use.

  Also, a Mistborn can ingest a very rare metal called Lerasium, which turns them into an Original Mistborn. Original Mistborns get slightly lower fuel consumption, special night idle mist swirling effects, and
  most importantly, one Master Skill they can learn.
